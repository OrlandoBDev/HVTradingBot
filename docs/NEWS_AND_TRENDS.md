# News, Economic Calendar and Cross-Market Trends

The engine reads market news, the economic calendar and the trend across currency pairs, and uses them in three
places: the trade score, the risk engine and learning. All of it is deterministic and auditable, and it can only make
trading **more careful**: it never raises a risk limit, never grows a position and never bypasses the kill switch.

## Sources (`News:Provider`)

| Provider | What it reads | Used when |
|---|---|---|
| `Public` (default) | Forex Factory weekly calendar export (`News:CalendarUrl`, JSON) and RSS/Atom headline feeds (`News:HeadlineFeeds`). Free, no API key. | Real market data (Deriv). |
| `Simulated` | A generated calendar and headlines, reproducible from the seed and the time. Follows market time. | Automatically with `MarketData:Provider=Simulated` (`./run.sh dev`), and for offline development. |
| `None` | Nothing. | Switched off (`News:Enabled=false` has the same effect). |

The calendar is cached for an hour. Headlines refresh every `News:RefreshMinutes` (15). A failed refresh keeps the last
calendar for up to a day and is retried after a minute; news problems never stop the engine. If the calendar cannot
be read at all, trading continues without it, unless `News:BlockWhenCalendarUnavailable` is `true`.

Backtests have no news (past headlines are not stored); the cross-market trend still applies, computed from the
replayed bars, exactly as live.

## Which markets news affects

- **Currency pairs:** releases and headlines for both currencies (EUR/USD: EUR and USD).
- **Metals, crypto, stock indices:** releases for the pricing currency (USD).
- **Derived (synthetic) markets:** none. They are generated by Deriv and are not moved by real-world news.

## Risk engine (`NewsEvents` rule)

| Situation | Effect |
|---|---|
| High-impact release on the market's currency from `BlackoutMinutesBefore` (30) before to `BlackoutMinutesAfter` (30) after | **Rejected**: no new trade. |
| Medium/high-impact release within `CautionMinutes` (120) | Position size × `CautionRiskMultiplier` (0.5). |
| Recent headlines clearly against the trade (net sentiment ≤ −`SentimentThreshold`) | Position size × `OpposedSentimentRiskMultiplier` (0.75). |

Multipliers combine (0.5 × 0.75) and are capped at 1, so news can only shrink a position. Test trades go through the
same rule. Every other risk rule, the kill switch and the double risk check are unchanged.

## Trade score

Three bounded adjustments are added to the 0–100 score and shown on every decision:

| Adjustment | Range | From |
|---|---:|---|
| `NewsSentiment` | −6 … +3 | Time-weighted headline sentiment for the pair (half-life 6h, last 24h, at least 3 headlines). |
| `MarketTrend` | −6 … +3 | Cross-market currency trend: each pair's 4H EMA20−EMA50 gap in ATRs counts for its base and against its quote currency; a currency needs at least two pairs. Trading the strong currency against the weak one scores higher. |
| `ContextLearned` | −6 … +3 | What learning found these news and trend conditions to be worth (below). |

Penalties are larger than boosts on purpose. Headline sentiment is a small word list (e.g. "hawkish", "beats",
"slumps", "EUR/USD rises"), so it is predictable and easy to audit rather than clever.

## Learning

Every setup is recorded with its news condition (`Calm`, `EventRisk`, `SentimentWith`, `SentimentAgainst`) and its
trend alignment (`With`, `Against`, `Neutral`). When it resolves, learning measures each condition against the same
strategy's average under all conditions, shrunk towards zero like the per-strategy model:
`excess = Σ(R − strategy average) / (n + PriorStrength)`. So a strategy that is poor everywhere is not penalised twice;
only the difference the news or trend makes moves the score, within `Learning:ContextMaxPenalty` (6) and
`Learning:ContextMaxBoost` (3). The Learning page lists what has been learned; the News page shows the calendar,
headlines and what they mean for each selected market now.

## Look-ahead

Only headlines published at or before the evaluation time are used. Calendar events are scheduled in advance, so
knowing a future release time is not look-ahead.
